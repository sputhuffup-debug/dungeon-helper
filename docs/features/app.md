# App features

[Campaign Forge](../../README.md) · [App features](app.md) · [Alien RPG](alien.md) · [Fifth edition](dnd5e.md) · [Remaster (ORC)](orc.md)

What Campaign Forge does for every game system. Features that belong to one game module are on that module's page.

## What it does

- **Local-first PWA.** The campaign lives in the browser (IndexedDB), works offline at the table, and exports to a self-describing zip bundle at any time. No server, no accounts.
- **One place per game system.** The campaign list groups everything by game module: start a campaign, load its demos and open its campaigns from that system's section.
- **Set up your campaign.** A new campaign's Home page leads with an ordered checklist to its first session: each step links straight to where it is done and ticks itself from the campaign's data (never from a click), with "3 of 8 done" progress. Every game system gets the generic steps (player characters, players and who plays whom, the first session); a module or data pack adds its own and reorders them per play mode (`setup`). *Hide this* turns it off and Settings shows it again; it disappears once every step is done. Modules can also declare campaign settings the New campaign dialog asks once (`campaignSettings`), which same-named generator inputs then start from, and pickers that would dead-end on "none yet" (the crew's vehicle, a home base) offer *+ New…* to make one from a preset and choose it in one go. See [Setting up a campaign](../modules/setup.md).
- **Guide page.** A module can ship a step-by-step guide from a new campaign to its first session (`guide`). The Guide page shows it chapter by chapter, each step with what to click, why, and a screenshot of that panel, and "Take me there" buttons that open that page of the campaign (an "Open the job" link waits until there is a job). *Look it up* links beside them open the Reference page at what the step is about: a list or one of its items, a roll table, or a preset. Reach it from Home, the setup checklist (*Step-by-step guide*, even once the checklist is hidden or done, through Home) and, before any campaign, the module's *Guide* on the campaign list. Text and images are built into the app and precached, so it reads offline; the same text is generated into a docs page ([Your first Alien campaign](../guides/alien-first-campaign.md)). See [The GM's guide](../modules/guides.md).
- **Campaign structure.** Adventures, acts, scenes, sessions, timeline, handouts, notes with `@` entity references and backlinks.
- **World entities.** Characters, creatures, locations, factions, items, vehicles, ships, clues, jobs and encounters, each rendered from the module's JSON Schema sheet layout, linked by typed relationships.
- **Character builder.** Modules and data packs can declare a step-by-step builder for their player characters (`characterBuilders`): choices over reference lists, fixed options or values worked out from earlier picks, with grants that write the sheet's stored fields and the sub-choices a pick brings; an ability score step (arrays, point buy, rolls in the app or dice typed from the table, typed scores); typed fields; a review with the sheet preview and every grant beside its source; and level-up steps for an existing character. *Build a character* appears on the Atlas PCs tab and the new-entity page, *Level up* on a character's sheet; unfinished drafts are kept in the campaign and resumed from the PCs tab. Everything is JSONLogic, so a pack declares it without code. See [Character builder](../modules/character-builder.md).
- **Characters from a generator.** A module or data pack can mark a procedure as one that makes a character (`createsCharacter`, e.g. a life path): its button sits beside *+ New* on the Atlas's PCs tab, in the tab's empty state and on the new-entity page while it makes a player character. Generator previews read fields as the sheet shows them: reference list ids by their item's name, talents with their count, gear and weapons by name and quantity, references by name; the raw results stay behind each step's *Raw result*. See [Tables and procedures](../modules/tables-procedures.md#procedures-that-make-a-character).
- **A gear list and crewmate pickers.** A `gear` sheet widget can offer *From the gear list…* (`catalog`): a searchable, grouped picker over the module's item presets with their cost, weight and page, which sends a weapon to the sheet's weapons list with its stats and anything else to the gear (raising the quantity of an item already there); generators use the same placement for the kit they hand out. A `ref` field can be a dropdown of the campaign's other player characters (`pick: "select"`, `pc`), stored as a reference and shown by name, with a name typed in an older campaign kept and linked in one click. See [Sheets and widgets](../modules/sheets.md#the-gear-list).
- **Weapons and starting details.** A `weapons` sheet widget any module or data pack can use: weapons with their stats as columns, an *Add weapon* picker over the module's presets (filtered and grouped from the sheet's config), links to item entities in the campaign, custom rows edited inline, and their weight counted with the gear. With an attack config, table mode rolls an attack with a weapon and applies its damage. The new-entity form also asks for the fields a sheet marks for creation (`sheet.create`), such as a character's starting weapons. See [Sheets and widgets](../modules/sheets.md#the-weapons-widget).
- **Generators.** Seeded, re-rollable procedures over the module's tables: world creation, star systems, jobs, life paths, encounter checks, shore leave and between jobs, with full roll provenance.
- **Generators that read the campaign.** A procedure can ask for the campaign itself as an input: a snapshot of its entities (names, kinds, tags and the scalar fields it names) and their relationships, or an adventure picked from a list (premise, hidden situation, acts, cast and site map rooms), whose PCs then fill the per-character inputs. The snapshot is stored with the run, so a run replays the same. Steps can split their result into parts (one slot of one card) that the GM re-rolls or pins one at a time, and procedures that draft per-act agendas get editable draft cards with an accept that writes each character's agendas and the adventure's agenda cards, asking before it replaces text the GM wrote. Procedures that keep long-term agendas (a goal over several jobs, a beat per job, open friction) get the same cards, an accept that writes each character's goal and a GM-only agenda handout and a players' friction handout per character, an Agenda tab on each PC, and a per-job step in the session wrap-up that deals the next beats. Procedures that deal a board of offers (a job board) get one card per offer, with what the players will read beside what only the GM knows, each offer and each of its parts re-rolled or pinned on its own, and its own accept, keep or discard: accepting adds the offer's records at the visibility the module gives each (a contact for the players, the rest GM-only) and a players' handout; a job then shows its people, handouts and the site brief a map generator reads, with *Generate site map*. See [Tables and procedures](../modules/tables-procedures.md#inputs-from-the-campaign).
- **Table tools.** Scene cards, rounds/stretches/shifts clock, stress and panic roller, critical injuries (including the android table), initiative cards, supply rolls, countdowns, a xenomorph life-cycle tracker and a session checklist that modules extend. A Hints tab says what each attribute and skill is for in the story and rolls it for the best-placed PC.
- **Table mode on a phone or tablet.** Below desktop width table mode is full-height panels behind a bottom tab bar (Scene, Map, Crew, Dice, Log), so the map, the dice and stress are one tap away instead of screens of scrolling; the header keeps the timer and a menu (player screen, shares, end session), and the clock scrolls in one line. The map opens framed on the zones the crew has seen and stands in (*Focus*; *Fit* shows the whole deck), deck plan first and its tools under it. On a desktop the live map sits high in the left column. Participants are compact rows everywhere: name, stress pips, health with *Take a hit*, a creature's turn, and *More* for the stats, rolls, attacks, story points and life cycles, so the trackers are not repeated.
- **One round counter.** The header clock is the table's only round: the hunt's *Next round* and *Next stretch* and the initiative tracker's *Next round* advance it and read from it (initiative's *Next round* still lets everyone act again), so the header, the hunt and initiative never disagree.
- **Safer table controls.** *Reveal all* on the live map can be undone from its toast; *Reset the map* sits under *More map actions*, asks first and can be undone. *Take a hit* has one primary action: its footer reads *Cancel* until a hit is applied, and closing with a hit set up but not applied asks first. Repeated identical lines in the event log show once with a count, a toast pushed again counts up instead of stacking (three at most), and tokens on the player map show two letters ("RV" for Ripley-Vance), with the short name under a token alone in its zone.
- **The record at the table.** When a module runs a record as an adventure (a job), table mode shows *This job* (GM only) for its scenes and for scenes at its site: the arc clues seeded in it or with its people, each with *Mark found* and *Reveal* (found by the crew and shown on the player screen by its crew-notes text only, until *Off the screen*), and the module's notes on each of its people (`AdventureSourceDef.people`: an agenda, a secret, whether they turn on the crew). None of it reaches the player screen, shares or packets. The record's page shows the same notes under its people, and a place's sheet names the saved site map whose location it is instead of "No map". See [Adventure sources](../modules/adventure-sources.md#the-records-people-people).
- **Combat for d20 games.** Modules and data packs that declare their d20 combat (where a combatant's AC, hit points, saves, damage modifiers and attacks live, how attacks and saves resolve, their conditions) get a combat panel in table mode: the initiative order with the current turn (*Next turn* ticks condition durations, end-of-turn decrements and save-ends saves), hit points with damage, healing and temporary hit points, AC and speed with their conditions, and condition chips whose helper text says what each condition does, what the app applies by itself, what it leaves to the GM, how long it lasts and where it came from. Attacks pick an attacker, one of its attacks (from a stat block or a sheet) and a target, roll with advantage or disadvantage from the conditions on both sides (the reasons shown, the GM can override), say hit, miss or critical hit and why, roll damage (critical hits as the module says) and apply it through the target's resistances, weaknesses and immunities, with the module's rule at 0 hit points; save effects roll each target's save against the DC for full or half damage and conditions. Checks and saves rolled from a sheet carry the conditions' modifiers. Every roll can be typed from the table; everything is logged for the GM only, and the player screen shows only how hurt the visible combatants are.
- **Skill rolls you can adjust.** Table mode's skill roll keeps the character while the GM adds or removes dice: a − / + modifier stepper (any dice system's small whole-number roll option shows as one) and, when the module says which gear helps which skill (`skillBonus` on the sheet's gear list), a *Gear* pick of the items on that character's sheet that change the roll, a hindering suit included. Both go into the pool shown before the roll and into the roll log's line ("Lee-Ann Jenkins: Medical Aid (Personal medkit +2, Modifier +1)"), so there is no need to fall back to a free roll. See [Sheets and widgets](../modules/sheets.md#the-gear-list).
- **What the player screen shows.** The GM decides: by default the player screen shows only the handouts revealed this session, plus any the GM pins. Table mode's Reveal card lists what is on the screen now, with *Clear the screen* (also beside *Player screen* in the header and in the phone menu; it takes the reveals, the recap and a clue in the spotlight off, with Undo on its toast), *Pin* and *Unpin* per handout, *Show again* for one revealed earlier, and the other handouts the players may already see (a job's posting, the crew's frictions) to pin when they matter. Pinned handouts stay through a clear and into the next session. The leak protections are unchanged: a handout the module keeps with the GM never shows, pinned or not, and a personal handout reaches its player only through *Give to player*. The player screen leaves out rulebook page refs.
- **Dice rolled at the table.** For groups who roll physical dice, every roll can take the faces instead: the roll panel, table mode's one-tap rolls (stress, panic, supply, critical injuries, broken and life-cycle checks, creature attacks and reactions, special attacks, duct events, prompt answers), initiative, travel checks and each generator step. One box per die in the order the rules read them, labelled from the dice system (base and stress dice, a d20 pair for advantage, a push's re-rolled dice only), with a 2d6 total, a d66 or a percentile typed as one number; faces the dice cannot show are refused inline. Outcomes, pushes, table rows and effects are exactly an app roll's, and the log marks the roll "entered" with its faces. A switch on each roll surface and in table mode flips between rolling in the app and entering dice; *I roll physical dice* in Settings makes entering the default on that device.
- **Share with players.** From table mode or a session page, send the player view to the group chat without a server: phone-sized cards (the scene briefing, the map with fog exactly as the player screen shows it, each revealed handout, an optional party card), one black-and-white PDF packet in Letter or A4, or one self-contained web page. Built from the player projection only (no GM notes, hidden zones, GM map layers, unrevealed or per-player handouts, or GM-only fields), drawn on the device, then handed to the phone's share sheet (Messages, Messenger, WhatsApp) or downloaded as a zip on desktops. Each share is recorded in the session, so the next one can send only what is new, with a ready-to-paste chat message.
- **Players and player packets.** Settings lists the players at the table: a name, a contact note for the GM and the PC or PCs each plays (a PC's sheet shows and sets its player). Visibility on entities, handouts and single fields (*Field visibility…* on a sheet) can name chosen players, and a handout written for a character can be given to its player. *Share with players → Player packets* then makes each player their own copy to send them alone: what every player may see, what was shared with them, and their own character's sheet and agenda with the fields the module shows a character's own player (`"x-visibility": "owner"` in the schema, `ownerHandouts` for handout kinds). Each player's preview is exactly their file, as phone images, a PDF or one self-contained web page titled "for <player>", one player at a time or all of them as a zip, with what changed since their last packet. Packets are built through that player's projection only, so GM notes, hidden map areas, GM-only fields and another player's private material never reach them; the group share still leaves every per-player item out. In table mode a handout written for one character offers *Give to <player>* instead of a one-tap reveal, showing it to everyone asks first, a revealed handout can be hidden again, and a handout the module keeps with the GM (the GM's own agenda card) has no reveal at all and never reaches a player even if marked revealed.
- **Preview as player, page by page.** *Preview as player* on the Atlas carries into every entity page it opens (and *More… → Preview as player* on any entity page): the page is drawn from the player projection only, the way a player packet shows a sheet, so GM-only fields, whole GM sections, notes, GM-only links and machine tags are left out. It is read-only, under a banner with *Exit preview*; links to other entities keep the preview, and an entity players cannot see says so.
- **Readable values, raw ids kept back.** Selects, read-only sheets, Atlas summaries, generator previews and packets name an enum value by its label (`enumLabels` beside `enum` in the schema), procedure inputs by their titles, and the Generate breadcrumb names the procedure. A step's line is its result's label, effects worded as the Effects list words them, ids left out; the whole result stays under *Raw result*. Machine tags (`within:…`, `subtype:…`) stay out of the Atlas tag filter and its tag column. A sheet section can say which entities it is for (`when`), so a PC's sheet has no NPC dossier, and an entity page keeps its less used actions (preview, a life cycle's start, delete) under *More…*. See [Sheets and widgets](../modules/sheets.md#layout).
- **"Previously on…" recaps.** A planned or running session's page opens with a recap of what the campaign recorded since the last session (or the last two or three sessions, or the story so far), assembled from templated, original sentences with no AI: same campaign, same recap. The GM's version has the sessions covered with their summaries and a line from each log, scenes run, what was revealed, who and where the crew met, rewards, who played, the wrap-up's answers, loose ends, and a "What the players don't know" section (clues placed but not found, and whatever the module adds). The read-aloud for the players is built apart, from the player projection only, so it never holds a GM title, sponsor, unfound clue, clock, heat, GM-only record or field, or hidden agenda. Every line links its records and can be switched off or rewritten, and the GM can add lines; *Regenerate* rebuilds from the data and keeps those choices. The read-aloud's "Last time" names the session with its in-world date (the campaign's date when the session started), never the table's real-world date, and offers the GM's wrap-up summary as a line marked *your words* that starts switched off, since it is the GM's own text; switching it on (and back off) survives *Regenerate*. *Show on player screen* puts the read-aloud on the players' window (and ticks "Start with a recap" on the checklist); it is also an optional "Previously on…" section of the group share and each player's packet. The recap is stored on the session and travels in bundles; modules add their own lines through `recap` contributors. See [Session recaps](../modules/recaps.md).
- **Campaign primer.** A player-facing introduction for session 0 (the pitch, the setting, what everyone knows, places and factions, tone and safety tools, house rules, making characters, where we begin), edited as rich text with the module's prompts and starter text in original words. *Draft from campaign* fills sections from player-visible places, factions, timeline, characters and the adventure's player intro, never from GM-only records or fields. It is linked from campaign home and session 0, and shared like a session: one phone card per section or pages of the PDF packet. Modules and data packs can supply their own prompts (`primer` in the contract and the pack format).
- **Run a record as an adventure.** A module can declare which records can be run as an adventure (`adventureSources`, e.g. a job): *Run this job* on the record (and beside an accepted offer on a board) makes the adventure from the module's template, filled from the record and what links to it (the player intro, the GM's situation, scenes set at its site, on its saved map, with its people), links the two both ways, and plans the first scenes into the session being played, else the next planned one, else a new one. Running it again opens the same adventure. *Adventures → New* offers the same as a picker under the template. See [Adventure sources](../modules/adventure-sources.md).
- **Template guidance stays with the GM.** In a module's templates, a paragraph in underscores is guidance: an empty template field shows it as placeholder text, and it is never written into the adventure, so it never reaches a scene title, the player screen, a share or a packet. Scene titles come from the template's own short titles, and templates may not name raw type ids.
- **Keep what you dealt.** Every generator page is the procedure's draft, kept in the campaign: the seed, inputs, re-rolls, pins, dice typed from the table and your own edits are saved as they change, so leaving and coming back (or reloading) shows the same board, agenda set or run, saying so, with *New deal* (which asks first when the draft has your work on it) as the only way to deal afresh. One draft per procedure per campaign; *Recent runs* lists open drafts, marked as drafts, beside accepted runs. On a board of offers, taking one marks it on the board (kept as dealt, linked to the record it made, with opening that record as its next step) instead of leaving the board; the others can be taken later or all kept as leads at once. Once a run is accepted the main button is the next step (open what it made, the agenda, the arc board) and *Accept again* is a quiet button whose confirm says it makes duplicates; reopening an accepted page says so. Drafts are GM-only (the player projection holds none, so they never reach shares, packets or the player screen) and travel in bundles with the rest of the campaign.
- **A wrap-up that keeps what you type.** Every wrap-up field (the summary, free rewards, timeline entries, loose ends, the award ticks and the step you were on) is saved on the session as you type, so leaving for the table or reloading loses nothing; answers and closing a job save when made, and *Finish session* is still what commits. An empty summary is drafted from what the session logged, in templated sentences with no AI (the scenes run, clues found, what the players were shown, panic, injuries and damage, rolls of note, motion-tracker sweeps and threats seen, then your own log lines), marked *Draft* until you edit it, with *Draft again from the log*. The end-of-session questions step holds the module's prompts with its award checklist (and the arcs), and is left out when the module asks nothing there.
- **Close what you ran.** When a session ran a record its module closes after play (a job), the wrap-up ends with a step to close it: the outcome (done, failed, carried over), its payout with an undo, its adventure marked done, and the module's between-jobs procedure with the record picked. *Finish session* lands there, or on the next session's page; table mode then skips the scenes of finished adventures. A procedure that creates nothing but carries effects (shore leave, between jobs) applies the effects you leave ticked when you accept it, never one the ledger already holds, moves the arcs' clocks in the same step when it is the between-jobs procedure, and ends with *Deal the next job board* and *Plan the next session*.
- **The recap at the table.** Table mode opens a session that has a recap (or a played session before it) with a compact "Previously on…" at the top: the read-aloud's first lines, *Show to the players* (on the player screen) or *Hide from the players*, or *Build the recap* when there is none yet. It goes away when the first scene is marked run, or when the GM dismisses it.
- **Printed booklets.** A black-and-white GM booklet (Letter or A4) with a title page, cast cards, locations and puzzles, the site map and each act's scenes with boxed read-aloud text, plus cut-out agenda cards for the players.
- **GM screen.** A printable quick reference for every game system, built from the module's own rule cards, tables, lists, conditions and play guide (*GM screen* on Home, or *Rules → GM screen*). *On screen* shows the panels you picked as cards to read at the table, GM side then player side, each block with its page; *Print* lays them out as a landscape tri-fold with the GM side and a player side, on Letter, A4, 11×17 or A3, or one card per panel for a binder. Pick the panels, their order and size; the preview shows both sides at scale; type shrinks to fit, table rows never split, and anything left out is listed. Vector PDF in the booklet's black and white, with the module's licence notice and attribution and every block's page or section. Modules and data packs declare it as `gmScreen`; one without gets a screen from its rule cards. See [GM screen](../modules/gm-screen.md).
- **Campaign arcs.** Modules can declare an arc board (`arcs`): an Arcs page where threads are columns, stages rows and clues cards marked unseen, seeded or found, linked to their jobs, NPCs, places and sessions, with faction heat and thread clocks on top, a "what the crew knows" filter, and everything editable (titles, stage order, clues, clocks by hand). A generator deals threads as offers; boards with an arcs input seed clues into what they deal and let heat, clocks and contacts weight it, saying on each offer what influenced it. Clues are marked found from the job, the NPC, the board or the session wrap-up, which also asks who noticed the crew and which NPCs to keep as contacts; clocks tick in the between-jobs flow by a seeded roll or by hand. Contacts keep a stance, favours, grudges and what they know on the NPC. The players get crew notes only (found clues under each thread's player title, met contacts, the places and factions they know), on the player screen and as an optional section of the group share and each player's packet; threads are GM-only, travel in bundles and never reach a player projection. See [Campaign arcs](../modules/arcs.md).
- **Live hunts on site maps.** A map generator can declare a hunt (`hunt`: movers, sensors, steps): table mode's live map then holds hidden threat tokens the GM places from the map's marks or the cast and moves zone by zone, with a seeded suggested move and an alternative for each from the module's data-driven weights (never applied without the GM), a faint GM-only trail, and sensor sweeps by the PCs that carry one: lettered blips at a range band and a direction, never a zone, logged and shown on the player screen as a tracker readout until the GM reveals the threat on the player map. Advancing the table clock (from the header, the hunt or the initiative tracker: one round counter) asks the GM to move threats and sweep. The placement picker lists the map's marks, then *NPCs on this map* (one entry per person across acts, with their record), then the rest of the cast. Hidden tokens, trails and suggestions never reach the player screen, shares or packets. See [Map generators](../modules/map-generators.md#the-live-hunt-typescript-generators).
- **Dungeon map generator for any module.** A built-in map engine a data pack configures as data (`mapGenerators` with `engine: "dungeon"`): site types over six floor plan archetypes (halls, caverns, tunnels, keep, channels, nave), one or more levels joined by stairs, doors, locked and secret doors, portcullises and collapses, room purposes and read-aloud text from the module's tables, encounters, traps and treasure picked from its own presets, lists and tables by party level and threat. Staged re-rolls (floor plan, doors, dressing, stocking), block checks on every map (way in, every room reachable, levels linked, no overlaps), and saving can add the site, its rooms, encounters and creatures to the campaign and show the map on the adventure's scenes there. The players see only the rooms the GM reveals (no outline of the rest, no secret doors or traps until found), on the player screen, in shares and bundles; prints at 1 inch = 5 feet with 5-foot squares. See [Map generators](../modules/map-generators.md).
- **Maps from images.** Imported images with rectangular zone overlays and open/blocked/door borders.
- **Rules reference.** Rule cards filtered by play mode: paraphrases with page references for Alien, the source's own text cited by section for the fifth edition and ORC modules.
- **Search finds rules and reference entries.** The header search lists the module's rule cards for the campaign's play mode under *Rules*, and what the Reference page lists (reference list items such as spells, talents and gear, entity presets, roll tables) under *Reference*, beside the campaign's records, each with its page or section and, for a reference entry, its list. Opening a rule shows its card on the Rules page; opening a reference entry opens it on the Reference page: an item in its list's dialog, a table in the table dialog, a preset on the Presets tab, filtered to it and marked. The Reference page's tab and open entry are in its address, so a link opens the same entry.
- **Multi-system modules.** Every game system is a module: a TypeScript package, or a declarative JSON data pack (no code) that the SDK turns into one. GMs import packs in the app (*Game modules…* on the campaign list); a pack is validated before it is stored, never runs as code, shows its licence and attribution, and travels inside campaign bundles. Packs pick one of four built-in dice engines: d20 (advantage, disadvantage, DC, optional four degrees of success with natural 20/1 shifts, situational modifier choices), dice pool, 2d6 bands and d100 roll-under. `pnpm create-module` scaffolds a pack or a TypeScript module and `pnpm module-check` validates one or `--all`; the author guide is [docs/modules](../modules/README.md).
- **Supplements.** Extra options for a module you already have (backgrounds, subclasses, races, feats, spells, presets, tables, rule cards) as one JSON file of additive data, imported under *Game modules…*. A supplement is checked against the module it extends, merged in when that module loads, listed under it with its notice, marked with a source badge wherever its entries appear, recorded in generator provenance and carried in campaign bundles. Sheet fields suggest list items as you type (the fifth edition sheet's race, subrace, background and subclass), supplement items included, and still take any text. `pnpm create-module --supplement <module>` scaffolds one; [docs/modules/supplements.md](../modules/supplements.md) covers the format and keeping private supplements private.
- **Built-in supplements.** Supplements can ship with the app, optional per device: *Remaster ORC books* adds the ORC content of nine more remaster books to the ORC core rules module (820 creatures and NPCs, 27 hazards, 12 classes, 8 ancestries, feats, spells and items, still without the setting's names). It is listed under its module in *Game modules…* with *Built-in* and *Supplement* badges and an on/off switch (off by default; it roughly doubles the module's data), merged in when the module loads while it is on, marked with its source badge, and named, not embedded, in exported bundles, so importing one switches it on. A large supplement can be split into a main file and parts.
