# Alien RPG (Evolved Edition)

[Campaign Forge](../../README.md) · [App features](app.md) · [Alien RPG](alien.md) · [Fifth edition](dnd5e.md) · [Remaster (ORC)](orc.md)

The `alien` module (`packages/module-alien`): a companion to the Free League rulebook, not a replacement. It encodes structure, tables and mechanics as data and cites the page; it does not reproduce the book's prose or art. The data has been checked against the printed Evolved Edition core rules; [`docs/verification/`](../verification/README.md) records what was corrected.

Demos: *Skyfire Down* (campaign play, with an infection under way, the crew's long-term agendas one job in, a job board whose taken posting has three NPCs, a betrayer and a generated site map with a live hunt under way, an arc board with two threads, a clue found in session 1 and Mara Quill as a contact, and four players, one note shared with one of them) and *Cold Harbor Relay* (a cinematic one-shot built with the guided builder, with a generated site map, duct levels and live map state ready for session 1).

## What it adds

- **Campaign setup.** The New campaign dialog asks the campaign frame once (Space Truckers, Colonial Marines or Frontier Colonists, p. 234-235), keeps it in the campaign's settings (changeable in Settings), and the job generator, the job board and between jobs start from it, still changeable per run. The setup guide on Home walks campaign play through the crew (*Roll a Life Path* or *+ Character*), the players, the crew's ship (*+ New spaceship…* on the Ship page makes one from a ship preset and chooses it), a home base, campaign agendas, campaign threads, a job board with a job taken, and *Run this job* for session 1; cinematic play gets a shorter list (the PCs, the players, the adventure, the act agendas, the session). Each step ticks from the campaign's data.
- **First campaign guide.** [Your first Alien campaign](../guides/alien-first-campaign.md), on the app's Guide page and as a docs page, walks campaign play from the New campaign dialog to the end of session 1, a screenshot for every step: the crew by Life Path and by hand, players and who plays whom, buddy and rival, the crew's ship, a station with a bar inside it and the home base, NPCs from a typical NPC preset and by hand, relationships, @ links and backlinks, the graph and who sees what, a job board, taking a job and its people, the site map, *Run this job*, a scene's place and people, an act's handout, then planning, running, revealing and wrapping up the session. In the app, *Look it up* links open the careers, talents, skills, frames and ship classes lists, the Bison and Ship’s engineer presets, and the stress response and panic tables on the Reference page. Its example campaign (Breakwater Freight) is original; the screenshot script does every step in the app.
- **Making the crew.** *Roll a Life Path* sits beside *+ New* on the Atlas's PCs tab (and in its empty state, and on the new-character page for a PC), opening the appendix's life path (p. 282-289). Its preview reads like the sheet: the career and talents by name, the gear with quantities, the weapons, the summary ("Career Officer · Stress 0 · Health 3"); each step's *Raw result* still holds the rolls, and the random general skills steps say which skills they picked. Accepting writes the origin, early life, company, experience, life event, career and psych evaluation into the PC's Background, beside the full provenance. The career gear (p. 31-39) is matched to the gear tables (p. 82-99): a weapon such as the M4A3 lands on the Weapons list with its stat line, so the weapons box and table-mode attacks work; suits, armor and the M314/M316 motion trackers become gear rows with their weight, power supply and preset (the live hunt still finds a tracker by name or preset), and lines the tables do not price stay plain gear. The 25 typical NPCs (p. 258-259) carry their weapons the same way, in the book's wording.
- **The gear list.** The Gear field's *From the gear list…* is a searchable picker of the gear tables' portable items (weapons, suits and armor, vision devices, diagnostics, tools, medical supplies, drugs, food and drink, data storage; vehicle guns, mainframes and fixed installations left out), each with cost, weight and page. *Add* puts a weapon on the Weapons list with its stats and anything else in Gear with its weight (one more of an item already there raises its quantity), and the list stays open saying where each item went. The tables' skill modifiers come with it to the table: a skill roll offers the PC's carried gear that changes it as a *Gear* pick (binoculars +2 OBSERVATION, a personal medkit +2 MEDICAL AID, the IRC Mk.50's −1 MOBILITY, p. 90-97), beside a modifier in base dice (p. 43).
- **Buddy and rival from the crew.** A PC's Buddy and Rival (campaign play, p. 27) are picked from the campaign's other PCs and stored as references, shown by name on the sheet, the player sheet and in packets. A name typed in an older campaign still shows (and counts) until the GM picks someone, with *Link to …* when a PC has that name. The wrap-up's XP questions (p. 30), the agenda drafts and the campaign agendas' buddy and rival lines read them, as well as buddy_of and rival_of relationships.
- **Guided adventure builder.** A step-by-step cinematic scenario builder (basics, situation, cast, locations and puzzles, three acts, review) with a field guide and page references on every step.
- **Agenda drafts.** A generator for cinematic play (no AI) that deals each PC one of 24 original three-act arcs (The Plant, The Debtor, The Protector, The Hidden Synthetic, The Mutineer…) weighted by a betrayal dial (loyal crew, frayed, knives out), and fills every slot from the campaign: who they answer to (the Company the adventure names, a rival corporation, the marshals, family, a belief, a synthetic's directive…), why, an objective per act aimed at the adventure's PCs (buddies and rivals), NPCs, key items, locations and site map rooms (fallbacks such as "the specimen" when the campaign has none), who to keep it from, and a catch. Objectives escalate from quiet in Acts 1-2 to open in Act 3 and aim at the climax, and each act notes how pursuing it earns the act's Story Point (risk or sacrifice, p. 158). A conflict check scores every pair (opposed objectives on the same target, competing masters, rivals), caps the party at one Hidden Synthetic and one Company plant, wants every PC in a clash and enough friction, and re-deals (seeded) when the deal fails, reporting each try. The drafts are marked DRAFT: re-roll or pin any slot, lock an arc, edit the text, then *Accept agendas* writes them onto each PC's sheet and the adventure's agenda cards, asking before it replaces text you wrote. Open it from the guided builder's PCs step or the Generate page.
- **Campaign agendas.** The campaign-play counterpart (no AI): each PC gets a long-term personal goal that lasts several jobs (24 original goals such as A stake in a ship, Union card, Make amends, The captain's chair), who they answer to, why, an embarrassing secret, and a fresh beat on every job: one concrete thing to do at a personal cost, with the bonus XP it can earn (p. 27, 30). The goal's one line stays on the sheet as the personal agenda, so the XP question and shore leave's Pursue Agenda (p. 257) keep working. Open friction lines, dealt by a friction dial (calm, simmering, prickly) and weighted per crew, tell every player whom they clash with and over what: competing for pay, berths and gear, clashing loyalties, embarrassing secrets that endanger no one, and buddies and rivals from the sheets. Friction, never betrayal: every goal, beat, line, secret and tension step is tagged level 0-1, and a no-betrayal check (no stance against a crewmate, no dangerous secret, tension under its cap, everyone in a clash, nobody the target of everything) plus a banned-words scan runs on every deal. Accepting writes each goal onto the sheet, a GM-only agenda handout and a players' friction handout per PC. After each job (in the session wrap-up, or *Deal next beats* on a PC's Agenda tab) mark each goal advanced, stalled or resolved: the next beat is dealt, a stall raises the tension one step (quiet disagreement, open argument, hard words, and no further), a resolved goal draws a follow-up that fits it, and the handouts update. A rule card covers agendas in campaign play and friction, not betrayal; the GM screen has an Agendas and friction panel.
- **Agendas reach their players.** In a player packet (*Share with players → Player packets*) each PC's player gets the sheet they play from (stats, stress, health, gear, XP, conditions) and their personal agenda with the campaign agenda's goal, this job's beat and the tension, even though all of it stays GM-only for everyone else; the agenda's embarrassing secret and history, the android box, the secret synthetic and NPC dossier fields and the other PCs' agendas stay out. The campaign agenda's GM card (with the secret) stays with the GM: it shows "Players get their agenda in their packet; this card stays with you." where the toggle would be. A cinematic agenda card goes to its player once the GM ticks *Give to <player>* on it (on the act's handouts or the guided builder's PCs step), so each act's agenda can be handed out when that act starts. The open friction handouts already go to everyone.
- **Job board.** Between jobs, deal one to six postings (campaign play, no AI): each is a job rolled exactly as the Job Generator rolls it (p. 244-252), dressed as the players read it (an original title, pitch, pay and terms, who posted it and where, deadline, stated requirements such as "Own ship required" or "No questions", and a risk rating that follows the difficulty but may undersell or oversell it), with the GM's side kept apart: the complications, the plot twist and what the employer really wants. An employer bias prefers corporations, governments or others; a confidential client stays GM-only. Each job comes with one NPC per point of difficulty (the book's D3, so up to three): the contact first, then roles weighted by frame and job (insider, rival crew lead, survivor, company liaison, stowaway, specialist, android), each with a name from the careers' lists, a stat line from the book's typical NPCs, a want, a secret, a tie to the job and a GM-only agenda that may betray the crew, reusing the agenda generator's arcs (The Plant, The Double Agent, The Hidden Synthetic…) plus original ones (The Setup, The Claim Jumper, The Informer). An NPC treachery dial (none, rare, common) weights them; the contact betrays less often; a job holds at most one hidden synthetic, whose android make stays GM-only. Every betrayer makes the job play one difficulty lower at its site (at least 1): the job shows "Difficulty 3 (plays as 2: one betrayer)". Each posting, and each part of it, re-rolls or pins on its own; take one (or keep several as leads) and the job, its employer, its NPCs (the contact visible to the players, the rest GM-only until met) and a players' posting handout are added. The board stays as dealt: a taken posting is marked on it with *Open the job* and *Run this job*, the others can be taken in a later session or kept as leads in one go, and the board is the same when you come back to it. The job page lists its people, the handout and its site brief.
- **Run this job.** An accepted job becomes a Job card adventure in one click (*Run this job* on the job page or beside the posting just taken; *Adventures → New → Job card* picks a job too): the posting (pitch, pay, terms, the contact) is the players' introduction; the complications, the plot twist, the employer's real intent, who turns on the crew, the NPCs' wants and secrets and the arc clue seeded in the job are the GM's situation; the arrival is set at the job's site, on its saved site map (or offers *Generate the site map* when there is none yet), with the job's NPCs and the clue to find as an event and as a GM-only Clue record in the scene's Clues, linked to the arc clue; the briefing has the contact, the complications land in their scenes, the payout says what to collect. The briefing, transit, arrival and first complication are planned into the next session. Template guidance stays placeholder text, so nothing of it reaches the players.
- **This job at the table.** For the job's scenes, and any scene at its site, table mode's *This job* (GM only) lists the arc clues seeded in the job or with its NPCs (a contact who knows something), each with *Mark found* and *Reveal* (on the player screen by its crew-notes line only), and each NPC's agenda, wants and secret from their sheet with whether they turn on the crew (*Turns on the crew* for the job's betrayers, p. 244). The job page shows the same notes under *People in this job*, and the site's sheet in the table drawer names its saved site map. Module-original wording.
- **Close the job.** The session wrap-up's last step for the jobs the session ran (or any accepted job not closed): done, failed (a new job status) or carried over, *Collect payment* with an undo, the trip in days (the job's distance at the ship's FTL rating, out and back, p. 108; one system away counts as a parsec, a module decision) with *Move the date* and an undo, the adventure marked done, and *Run between jobs* with the job picked, where *Finish session* lands. Accepting Between jobs applies its effects and moves the thread clocks in one go (untick what you don't want); a payment already collected is not listed again; the weeks in port move the in-world date on (7 days a week; a shore leave week run on its own moves it a week, one chained from Between jobs leaves it be), and the clocks box then shows the moves the run made. Then *Deal the next job board*.
- **Campaign arcs.** For long campaigns (campaign play, no AI), an arc board ties jobs into one story. *Deal threads* deals one to three threads, each from its own sponsor (a faction already in the campaign, cited at its setting page when it is one of the book's, or a minor corporation from the list on p. 162, added GM-only): a scheme from 16 original ones with a goal, one of 14 methods, one of 12 weaknesses and one of 10 costs of ignoring it; a GM title and a separate player title such as "The missing ore shipments"; three to five stages from the first hints to the endgame, each with two to four clues (a fact, an object, a person or a place, from 32 original templates) and what learning them unlocks; and a six to eight segment clock whose milestones say what the sponsor does next and suggest a payoff. Every part re-rolls or pins on its own. The job board then seeds the GM's clue (or, *auto*, the most advanced thread's next one) into one posting's material: the employer's real intent, an NPC's secret or agenda, a cargo detail, a complication, a key room of the site brief, or a contact who knows it; taking the posting records the clue as seeded with its job, posting and slot, and betrayers and hidden synthetics can work for a thread, which gives their agenda its sponsor and goal. Faction heat runs from Unnoticed to Hunted (0-6, GM-only): the wrap-up asks who noticed the crew this job, suggesting the job's employer and the factions it names. Between jobs each thread's clock rolls a D6 (1-2 nothing, 3-5 one segment, 6 two) and an ignored thread moves one more. Heat, clocks and contacts weight the next board (on by default, with a toggle): a hot faction's complications and its people at the site, an advancing thread's sponsor posting the job, a reached milestone's payoff, a contact posting the job by stance and favours, a surviving betrayer back with a grudge; each posting says what influenced it, and without arcs the board deals exactly what it did before. Surviving NPCs join the crew's contacts (stance, favours each way, grudges, the clues they know, where to find them, last seen), and the players read only their crew notes: found clues under each thread's player title, met contacts with where to find them and their favours, the places and factions they know, on the player screen and as a section of the group share and each player's packet.
- **Recap lines.** The module's contribution to the app's "Previously on…" recap, in original words: jobs taken and finished, clues found (under the GM's thread title for the GM, the thread's player title in the read-aloud), contacts gained with their favours and where to find them, crew funds and trips, and the live hunt (for the players, only threats revealed on their map). For the GM only: betrayers in play on the jobs in hand, hidden synthetics, each PC's agenda beat and how their goal went last job, thread clocks that moved between jobs, milestones and payoffs waiting, and faction heat with the change since the last recap. A synthetic or an NPC's agenda reaches the read-aloud only once the GM revealed it; long-term agenda secrets never do.
- **Site briefs and rogue humans.** Every board job carries a site brief for the map generator, read from its rolls: the site type from the destination and mission, size, decks and a threat budget from the effective difficulty, and a threat mix of xenomorphs, other creatures and rogue humans chosen from the mission, destination, complications and twist, plus hazards, win paths, key rooms and the job's NPCs (a betrayer marked to turn in Act 3). *Generate site map* opens the generator with the brief and saves the map with the job's site. Rogue humans are hostile groups built from the book's typical NPCs (a mercenary team, a UPP fire team, corporate security, a company cleaner, desperate colonists, cultists, claim jumpers, a hostile android) with a tier and cost against the act budget; they hold rooms, patrol corridors or guard the objective, never hide in ducts (androids may), appear on the GM map and in the zone key with their stat line, and never on the player map, its prints or a share.
- **Combat.** Creature turns roll or pick the creature's signature attack, rank targets by range from the live map and roll the attack dice; damage tracking with health bars, armor and broken PCs; reactions such as acid splash; special attacks (disarm, shove, grapple and more) with opposed rolls. Creature rolls stay GM-only.
- **Critical injuries and panic say what they do.** A rolled critical injury shows its effect, healing time (days, or one shift), whether it is lethal with the death roll's time limit, killed outright or needing surgery (p. 70); a panic roll shows its paraphrased effect and whether it calls for a trauma check (p. 72-74); stress responses, mental traumas and android system damage show theirs. The injury is written onto the PC sheet's *Critical injuries* and its badge on the participant row says the same on hover; *Roll Critical injury* is offered once per hit.
- **Secret synthetics stay secret.** An android posing as human (*Secret synthetic → Poses as human*, p. 25, 77), ticked by hand or made from the *Android, covert* preset (now a secret synthetic from the start, with cover scores capped at a human 5 and no android tag), shows the players its cover scores as its attributes and nothing of its true make: the android box, true attributes, skills, talents and the cover section stay GM-only by the module's own rule, wherever the players look (previews, packets, shares). *Field visibility…* marks those fields "Poses as human" with why.
- **Weapons on the character sheet.** A Weapons section beside Gear lists each weapon with its stat line as the gear tables print it (modifier, damage with E, stun or fire, range, ammo or power, features, weight), and the weapons count toward encumbrance with the gear. *Add weapon* opens a searchable picker of the book's personal weapons grouped ranged, melee and explosive (vehicle-mounted guns left out), each with modifier, damage, range, weight, cost and page; picking one copies its stats onto the sheet. *From campaign items* links a weapon item from the Atlas, and *Custom weapon* adds a row to fill in. A new PC or NPC created in the Atlas can pick starting weapons before it is saved. At the table, *Attack with …* (Dice tab, or the PC card's Attack button) rolls Close Combat at Adjacent range and Ranged Combat beyond, with the weapon's modifier and −2 per band inside its minimum range, shows the damage with extra successes, and applies the hit to a creature or NPC through its armor (armor piercing, stun and fire included); entered dice work too.
- **Ship and money.** A Ship page for campaign play: the crew's ship with its hull, component damage, spare parts, provisions, cargo and upkeep; crew funds summed from a ledger (add entries, filter by category, running balance, CSV export), and each PC's personal account read from their sheet's cash (an entry posted to a PC moves their cash, deleting it moves it back, so nothing counts twice; their ledger starts from what the sheet held); a travel planner that works out FTL time and the checks on arrival, then departs, rolls them and moves the in-world date; one-click job payments; and a Between jobs procedure for the pay, the lease, repairs and spare parts, food and water, then shore leave and the next job. Categories the book prices cite their page; docking, fuel and loans are plain GM bookkeeping.
- **Character progression.** An end-of-session award checklist in the wrap-up (the nine XP questions, one column per PC, totals, then Award) and Story Points per act in cinematic play; a Progression tab on each PC with the XP pool, Story Points, lifetime totals and a full history; Spend XP with the book's costs, the level 5 cap, career and general talents, stacking limits and the conditions for a new skill, shown with the dice pool before and after; undo of the last spend; Story Points spent at the table from the PC card or on a roll (one base die becomes a success). Every award and spend is logged per character and travels in the export bundle.
- **Site maps.** A seeded deck-plan generator that reads the adventure's locations, puzzles and crew, draws a floor plan in the shape of the site type (a starship's long spine, a derelict's broken hull, a station's ring, a colony's cluster of prefabs, a mine's winding adit, a lab's wings round an atrium, a ruin's great chambers), checks the map against the zone rules, and produces a GM map, a player map (fogged in play, whole in print), a tracking map, a zone key and a distance table. At the table the GM reveals zones, locks and seals doors, marks fires and pings the tracker; the player screen shows only what the crew has seen.
- **Duct levels.** Ducts are generated as separate maze sheets with labelled exits, shafts between levels, hazards and a GM-only duct event table rolled from table mode.
- **The live hunt.** On the live site map the GM places hidden threats from the map's spawns and rogue humans, a turning android or the job's NPCs, and moves them zone by zone on the hidden map (p. 56). The job's people are listed once each under *NPCs on this map* and move as *NPC* (an android NPC may use the ducts), never as hostile rogue humans; the recap says an NPC "has not met the crew on the map yet", not that they are hiding. Each stretch or round the app suggests where each goes, with an alternative: a Xenomorph one zone per point of Speed a stretch (p. 55) or two zones a fighting turn (p. 208), a facehugger swarm at its swarm Speed (p. 213), passive ones waiting (p. 55-56), through ducts and dark, cluttered rooms toward the nearest PC, falling back when hurt; rogue humans one zone a stretch, holding, guarding or patrolling, never in the ducts (androids may). The weights are the module's own and marked so; nothing moves until the GM accepts a move, picks another zone or moves the token on the map, and a faint GM-only trail shows where each has been. A PC carrying an M314 (Long, four zones) or M316 (Medium) motion tracker sweeps once a stretch (p. 58): everything within range that moved this stretch shows as a lettered blip, A-J like the Starter Set's signal tokens, at a range band and a compass bearing, never a zone; still threats show nothing, and a blip is not seeing them. Sweeps are logged ("Sweep by Lee-Ann Jenkins: blips A (Medium, NE), B (Long, N, above)"), the player screen shows a tracker readout, and when the crew sees the threat the GM reveals it: the blip flips to its token on the player map, keeping or retiring its letter. When the table clock passes a stretch or a round (the header, the hunt's own buttons and the initiative tracker all move the one clock), the GM is asked: move hidden threats? sweep? Skyfire Down's job site map has the stalker and the UPP fire team hidden and one sweep's blips on it (the scene *The ore run*).
- **GM screen.** Rolls and pushing, stress and panic (with the stress response and panic tables) and combat and damage on the GM side; your turn, gear and supply, and stress and recovery on the player side; hints, critical injuries, hazards and xenomorphs as extras; Story Points in cinematic play. Every block is one of the module's verified paraphrases with its page.
- **Labels, not ids.** Every value the module stores as an id has a label the app shows: frames (Space Truckers), job types and statuses, regions (Outer Rim), allegiances, careers, ship classes and ownership, clue kinds, colony sizes and every generator input's choices, and every generator input has a title (*PCs*, *Heavy Machinery dice*, *Living standard*). A PC's sheet has no NPC dossier (the appearance a career gives moved to Background), and *Start infection* sits under the entity page's *More…* instead of a red button at the top. Generated text has no template seams: a trucker posting's verb and pronoun agree with plural cargo ("Metal ingots or pellets go to…", "deliver them"), a thread's agent is "a …" where the crew first meets them and "the …" in the clues and milestones after, postings go up at the campaign's home base when it has one, a job never repeats a complication (a repeat takes the next row), and the PCs' first agenda beats look to different NPCs.
- **Wide-format printing.** Site maps print on 24" or 36" rolls as vector PDFs at 1 inch per metre (28mm miniatures), 28mm true scale or an overview scale, with a title block, scale bar and optional 1 m grid squares.

The app-wide features (table mode, hints, generators, sharing, booklets) work with this module too; see [App features](app.md).

**Add weapon**: the picker on a PC's sheet, grouped ranged, melee and explosive, with each weapon's modifier, damage, range, weight, cost and page; one click copies the stat line into the sheet's Weapons section.

![Add weapon picker](../screenshots/alien-weapons.png)

**Ship and money**: the crew's ship and its state, crew funds summed from the ledger with each PC's sheet cash as their account, Between jobs, and the travel planner with the days, NDD rolls, hypersleep and food the trip calls for.

![Ship page](../screenshots/ship.png)

**Wrap-up awards**: the nine end-of-session XP questions for every PC at once, with attendance ticked, questions that do not apply greyed out (no buddy, no rival) and each PC's new total before posting.

![Wrap-up award checklist](../screenshots/wrap-up-awards.png)

**Progression and Spend XP**: a PC's XP pool and history beside the Spend XP panel, with each skill's cost, its dice before and after, and the condition a new skill needs.

![Progression tab with Spend XP](../screenshots/progression.png)

**Site map generator**: the brief is filled from the adventure (key rooms, puzzles, NPCs) and every map is checked against the zone rules.

![Site map generator](../screenshots/map-generator.png)

**GM map**: zone numbers, ducts, spawns, clues, hazards, locks and act shading, with the legend and the rule each symbol carries.

![GM map](../screenshots/site-map-gm.png)

**Player map**: the same deck with every GM detail removed; it prints whole at 1 m = 1 cm, and at the table the player screen adds fog.

![Player map](../screenshots/site-map-player.png)

**Site types**: each site type has its own floor plan from the same brief: a starship along one spine, a derelict with breached and collapsed sections, a station ring round a hub, colony prefabs on open yards, a mine's winding adit, a lab's mirrored wings behind decon locks, and a ruin's great halls joined by galleries.

![Site types](../screenshots/site-types.png)

**Duct level**: each deck's ducts are generated as their own maze sheet, with runs, junctions, vent chambers and shafts that climb or drop between levels. Exits are labelled with the room they open into, generated hazards (vent fans, steam, flooding, collapses, drops) sit in the maze, and a GM-only duct event table rolls encounters, dangers and moments of respite at the table. Players see a duct zone only once the crew reaches it.

![Duct level](../screenshots/duct-map.png)

**Motion tracker**: the player screen during Skyfire Down's hunt: the crew on the revealed way in, and Lee-Ann's M314 sweep as the players see it, blips A and B on the range rings at their bearings, with no trace of where the threats are.

![The player screen's motion tracker readout with blips A and B](../screenshots/alien-motion-tracker.png)

**Hidden threats**: the GM's side of the same moment: the XX121 stalker and the UPP fire team as hidden tokens with their faint trails and suggested moves on the live map, each with its reasons, an alternative and the sweep buttons.

![The GM's live map with hidden threats, trails and suggested moves](../screenshots/alien-motion-tracker-gm.png)

**Roll print**: a 36" wide-format vector PDF at 1 inch per metre for 28mm miniatures, turned to fit the roll with all text upright, with a title block, 5 m scale bar and zone key in the margin. Decks too wide for the roll split into overlapping strips with cut lines.

![Roll print](../screenshots/roll-print.png)
